Confidence: strong = several studies agree; single = one study; weak = abstract/snippet only, secondary, uncited, or unverified. Tags: [D] = derived by me from the cited numbers (assumptions given); [R] = recalled standard/textbook value NOT re-opened this session (weak; verify before locking in). Compiled 2026-10-19 from Europe PMC abstracts, open full texts and search
  snippets; the shared web-search budget ran out mid-task, so some items stay [R] or snippet-only. Skin-surface kyphosis/lordosis is in back_sagittal_profile.md and not repeated here. Keys (Author Year) are in the reference list.

## 1. Structure and behaviour (my words)
- Build: every bone is a link with a mechanical axis and length L. A shaft is a thick-walled tube (outer radius R(s), canal radius r(s), cortical thickness t = R - r) with flared ends (thin cortical shell around a trabecular lattice) and joint surfaces. The spine is 24 presacral vertebrae (body + arch) separated by discs, so posture is the sum of small segmental angles
  (vertebral wedge + disc wedge). Ribs are curved bars on a pump-handle hinge; the pelvis is two innominates + sacrum around a canal. A joint is a centre + 1-3 axes + a range.
- Scaling: lengths follow stature (femur max length about 0.26-0.27 x stature [D]); diameters and endplate sizes follow length and sex (femoral head 48.5 mm men v 43.3 mm women, but the same isthmus canal); sex also flips a few shapes (subpubic angle 66 v 83 deg, femoral anteversion larger in women, pelvic canal shape). Spread within a sex is nearly as big as the sex gap (femur SD about 2 cm v gap about 3 cm).
- Material: cortical bone is stiff (E 14-18 GPa along the shaft, about 11 across), yields at 0.7-0.9 % strain, breaks at 2-3 %. Trabecular bone is a foam: modulus ~ density^3, strength ~ density^2. Load mode sets fracture shape: tension -> transverse/avulsion; axial compression -> impacted or oblique; bending -> transverse + butterfly wedge on the compressed side; torsion ->
  spiral, crack at about 45 deg to the axis (principal tension direction in pure shear) [D].
- Age 20-80: bones get wider (femur width +11 % men, +25 % women; vertebral area +15 %) while density falls (trabecular vBMD -46 to -55 % at central sites, cortical -18 to -25 %, cortical porosity rises); canal widens (+33 % in women 50-89 y); discs thin after the 4th decade; kyphosis +7 deg per 15 y after 65; stature -3/-5 cm (men/women, 30-70 y), -5/-8 cm (30-80 y); ribs flatten, cage rounder. Women lose more.
- Damage as parameters: a break = a plane (position s, normal, gap width, displacement vector, twist); callus = added spindle volume around the shaft; a compression fracture = lower anterior height Ha (wedge w = atan((Hp - Ha)/D)); a hunch = larger summed wedges; a scaled vertebra = scale factors on (Ha, Hp, W, D); a rotated tibia = twist between proximal and distal reference
  axes; weaker bone = smaller t and lower vBMD.

10. Healing: strain below 5 % favours intramembranous bone, below 15 % with pressure below -0.15 MPa endochondral (Claes 1999); callus index (max callus diameter / bone diameter) peaks at 14-27 weeks (Eastaugh-Waring 2009).

## 3. How to measure them on a 3-D mesh (derived recipes)
1. Long bone: centreline = centroids of slices normal to the principal axis. Max length = extent along the axis; mechanical axis = head centre to knee/ankle centre. Bow: chord c between 10 % and 90 % of length, sagitta s, radius R = c^2/(8 s) + s/2. Cross-section at 25/50/75 %: outer area A_o, canal area A_i, R = sqrt(A_o/pi), r = sqrt(A_i/pi), t = R - r, quadrant t by rays
  from the centroid; I_bend = pi/4 (R^4 - r^4) for an annulus (else polygon integral); circularity = 4 pi A/P^2.
2. Version/torsion: neck axis = head-centre to neck-isthmus centre; project it and the bicondylar line onto the plane normal to the shaft axis; anteversion = their angle. Tibial torsion = proximal posterior-condyle line v bimalleolar line, in the same plane. Neck-shaft = angle between neck axis and shaft axis in their common plane. Always store the definition: method changes the value by 5-10 deg (section 4.4).
3. Vertebra: fit endplate planes by least squares; Ha, Hm, Hp = distances between planes at anterior, middle, posterior edges; D, W = AP and transverse extents; body wedge = dihedral angle between endplate normals; disc height = distance between adjacent endplate centres along the mean normal; segmental angle = angle between adjacent facing endplate normals; Cobb (T4-T12 etc.)
  = angle between the end endplate normals = sum of segment angles [D].
4. Pelvis: landmarks ASIS, PSIS, symphysis, promontory, ischial spines/tuberosities; inlet AP (promontory to symphysis), inlet transverse (max), bispinous, subpubic angle (inferior pubic rami); PI = angle between the S1-endplate normal and the line from S1 midpoint to hip-centre; SS = S1 endplate v horizontal; PT = that line v vertical; PI = PT + SS identically [D].
5. Rib: centreline per rib; arc length, end-to-end distance, aspect ratio (height/length of the fitted plane curve), pump-handle angle to the spine; cage width/depth at T8 and its ratio.
6. Density: store vBMD per element; trabecular ROI = central two-thirds of the body inside the endplates (exclude the outer 2 mm shell, which carries 39-57 % of strength); BV/TV = rho_app / tissue density [D].
7. Fracture: fit axes to each fragment; angulation = axis angle in coronal and sagittal planes; translation = axis offset at the break as % of shaft diameter; shortening = lost overlap length; rotation = twist about the shaft axis; gap = minimum surface distance; callus index = max (bone + callus) diameter / bone diameter; callus volume = integral of (A_total - A_bone) ds;
  interfragmentary strain = gap displacement / gap width.

### 4.6 Density and mechanics
- Trabecular vBMD and vertebral strength (Christiansen 2011, Framingham QCT, 30 per group; FE strength, N): L3 young M 9647 +/- 2205 (vBMD 217 +/- 30), young F 8503 +/- 1413 (231 +/- 32), old M 7010 +/- 2735 (168 +/- 44), old F 4046 +/- 1091 (130 +/- 25); T10 young M 7759 (222), F 6473 (240), old M 6288 (184), F 3617 (148); outer 2 mm carries 42-43 / 39-43 % (young) and 47-57 %
  (old) of L3 / T10 strength: single, model-based.
- Riggs 2004 (373 F + 323 M, 20-97 y, QCT): young-men bone area 35-42 % larger; trabecular vBMD central -55 % F / -46 % M, peripheral -24 / -26 %; cortical vBMD -25 % F / -18 % M; vertebra and femur area +15 %: strong with Zebaze 2010 (122 women, 27-98 y): of 106.5 mg HA lost at the distal radius between 50 and 80 y, 68 % cortical and 32 % trabecular, 84 % after age 65;
  cortical density 80+ v 50-64 y is -43 % with porosity counted, -15 % without.
- Thresholds [R]: QCT L1-L2 trabecular above 120 mg/cm^3 normal, 80-120 low, under 80 osteoporotic (ACR); DXA T-score at or below -2.5 osteoporotic; young femoral neck aBMD about 0.86 (F) - 0.93 (M) g/cm^2 (Looker 1998, NHANES III 14,646 adults; numbers not in abstract).
- Densities: vertebral trabecular wet apparent density 0.09-0.35 g/cm^3, moduli 0.1-976 MPa, spread falls from about 400 % to 10 % under standardised testing (Ohman-Magi 2021 review: single); cortical wet apparent density 1.8-2.0 g/cm^3 and HA-equivalent cortical vBMD about 1100-1200 mg/cm^3 in young adults [R]; BV/TV = rho_app / (1.8-2.0) = 5-19 % [D]. Carter-Hayes 1977:
  strength ~ rho^2, modulus ~ rho^3, both ~ strain rate^0.06 (E = 3790 edot^0.06 rho^3 MPa, sigma = 68 edot^0.06 rho^2 MPa, rho in g/cm^3 [R coefficients]). Yield strain is nearly density-independent and site-specific (Morgan 2003 abstract; about 0.7-0.85 % [R]).
- Cortical (human femur, Reilly-Burstein 1975) [R]: longitudinal E 17.0 (tension) / 18.2 (compression) GPa, ultimate 135 / 205 MPa, ultimate strain 3.1 / 1.9 %; transverse E 11.5 GPa, ultimate 53 / 131 MPa; shear modulus 3.3 GPa, shear strength 65-70 MPa. Rib coupons (Kemper 2005; 117 tests, 6 donors, 18-67 y): E 13.9 GPa, yield 93.9 MPa at 0.88 %, ultimate 124.2 MPa at 2.7 %,
  plastic work about 60 % of strain energy; modulus up and peak strain down with age; no rib-level effect: single. Zioupos-Currey 1998 (male femur 35-92 y): modulus, strength, toughness fall steadily (percentages not retrieved). FE inputs in use: cortical 12,000 MPa, trabecular 200 MPa (weak).
### 4.7 Fracture loads, patterns, clinical measures, healing
- Femoral neck (sideways-fall set-up): femora of 74-y donors are half as strong and absorb one-third the energy of 33-y donors; load v neck aBMD r^2 = 0.92 (Courtney 1995, 8 old + 9 young); 50-fold faster loading adds about 20 % load and doubles stiffness; the estimated fall impact force is 35 % above the mean elderly fracture load (Courtney 1994); neck moment arm 7.3 % (F) /
  9.5 % (M) longer in fracture cases (Wang 2009, 63 cases, 57 controls). Absolute kN not retrieved.
- Femur shaft, dynamic 3-point bending: failure moment 458 +/- 95 Nm (15 femurs, 8 men; Funk 2004 snippet); impactor load 4780 +/- 792 N lateral-medial, 4310 +/- 1040 N posterior-anterior (45 tests; SAE 2004-22-0002 snippet); axial preload 4-16 kN + bending (Ivarsson 2009; 16 F + 23 M). Check [D]: R 14.5, r 6 mm (assumed) -> I = 33.7e3 mm^4, sigma = M R / I = 197 MPa, matching
  cortical bending strength near 200 MPa. Tibia torsion failure 130.6 +/- 53.2 Nm at 8.3 +/- 1.5 deg twist (11 proximal tibiae; Edwards 2012); 3-point bending 50 % risk 312 Nm (snippet, uncited: weak). Vertebra: FE strengths in 4.6; destructive data not retrieved. Whole-rib, radius FOOSH loads: not found.
- Patterns [R, with [D] geometry]: tension -> transverse/avulsion; axial compression -> metaphyseal impaction, diaphyseal oblique along the 45 deg shear plane, vertebral wedge (anterior column) or burst; bending -> transverse from the tension side + butterfly on the compression side; torsion -> spiral, 45 deg helix (one full turn = pi x diameter long, a half-turn spiral about
  1.6 diameters [D]); bending + compression -> short oblique; sideways falls load the femoral neck in bending + compression; usual low-trauma sites in osteoporosis: vertebral body, femoral neck, distal radius.
- Clinical measures: distal radius normals (Mignemi 2013, 185 fractures): volar tilt 11, radial inclination 22 deg, radial height 14 +/- 1 mm, ulnar variance 0 +/- 2 mm; (Dario 2014): 7-15 deg, 21-25 deg, 10-13 mm, 0.7-1.5 mm; volar tilt and ulnar variance matter most (single each). Tibia malunion thresholds (Patel 2022 review): 5-10 deg angulation, 1-2 cm shortening, 10-15 deg
  internal / 10-20 deg external rotation; angular malreduction defined as over 5 deg (Nork 2006). Humeral shaft acceptable: 20 deg anterior, 30 deg varus/valgus angulation, 3 cm shortening (Klenerman 1966) [R]. Femoral shaft rotation over 10-15 deg, shortening over 1-2 cm [R]. Femoral neck: Garden I-IV (valgus-impacted -> fully displaced) and Pauwels angle (line v horizontal)
  under 30 / 30-50 / over 50 deg [R]. Radial instability signs: dorsal angulation over 20 deg, dorsal comminution, intra-articular, age over 60 (Lafontaine 1989) [R].
- Healing timeline (Marsell-Einhorn 2011; Einhorn-Gerstenfeld 2015: about 10 % of fractures heal abnormally): hematoma/inflammation about days 0-7, cartilaginous soft callus about weeks 1-3, woven-bone hard callus about weeks 3-12, remodelling months to years [R timings]. Union typical: distal radius 6 wk, tibial shaft 16-20, femoral shaft 12-24 [R]. Callus index = max callus
  diameter / bone diameter, peaks at about 14 wk (cast), 22 (external fixator), 27 (nail) (Eastaugh-Waring 2009; index values not in abstract). RUST: scored per cortex (4 cortices, 1-4 each, total 4-16 [R]), ICC 0.86 inter-rater (Whelan 2010, 45 films, 7 raters). Mechanobiology: strain under 5 % and pressure under +/-0.15 MPa -> intramembranous; pressure below -0.15 MPa and
  strain under 15 % -> endochondral; otherwise fibrous tissue (Claes-Heigele 1999). Interfragmentary strain 2 % (bone) v 10 % (cartilage) [R, Perren 1979]; callus moduli granulation 0.2, fibrous 2, cartilage 10, woven 1000, mature bone 6000 MPa [R].

## 5. Suggested parameterisation from these numbers (all [D]; assumptions stated)
- Global inputs: stature H, sex, age, population offset. Long bone i: L_i = (H - b_i)/m_i from the Trotter-Gleser pairs (plus a random term of 0.8-1.0 SE), or f_i x H; sex ratios from Cho: femur length F/M 0.93 (stature 0.94), femoral head 0.89, vertebral widths 0.92-0.94, vertebral heights 0.91-0.98 (Greek CT and Singh extremes).
- Shaft section: canal diameter at midshaft about 12 mm (Cho mean of ML/AP, both sexes), mean cortex 6.9 mm (forensic snippet) -> R about 12.9 mm outer radius, r/R about 0.46; canal grows with age in women (+33 % from 50 to 89 y) and outer size grows too (Jung "femur width" +11 % M, +25 % F over life; its exact definition not seen), so re-derive t = R - r from both rather than assuming it. Anterior cortex thinnest, posterior thickest (5.1 v 7.7 mm).
- Spine: vertebra (Ha, Hp, W, D) from the level tables; disc heights from the MRI means; segmental angle_i = body wedge_i + disc wedge_i; Cobb = sum. Lordosis split [D, assumes "distal" = L4-S1 and the 68.6 % applies to an L1-S1 total of 55-60 deg]: L4-S1 about 38-41 deg, L1-L4 about 17-19 deg (about 6 deg per disc). Thoracic kyphosis T4-T12 34-41 deg = 4.3-5.1 deg per segment.
- Hunch / compression: extra kyphosis = sum over fractured levels of atan(dHa_i / D_i); e.g. six thoracic bodies each losing 1.5 mm anteriorly with D about 28 mm (assumed) add 6 x 3.1 = 18.4 deg. Age trend +0.47 deg/y after 65 (Kado), +6 deg across 40 y (see back_sagittal_profile.md). Genant grade sets dHa/Ha: 0.20-0.25, 0.25-0.40, over 0.40.
- Lordosis check: body wedges L1-L5 sum to -1.9 deg, so almost all lordosis is disc wedge; keep body wedge within the level table (+3.7 to -5.7 deg) and put the rest in the discs.
- Mass and strength checks: BV/TV = rho_app/(1.8-2.0); strength ~ rho^2, modulus ~ rho^3; vertebral strength L3 falls 9.6 -> 7.0 kN (men) and 8.5 -> 4.0 kN (women) from age 40 to 78 as trabecular vBMD falls 217 -> 168 and 231 -> 130 mg/cm^3 (so strength fell 27 % and 52 % while vBMD fell 23 % and 44 %: roughly proportional [D]). Annulus bending stress sigma = M R / I should land near 200 MPa at the reported failure moments (femur 458 Nm check above).
