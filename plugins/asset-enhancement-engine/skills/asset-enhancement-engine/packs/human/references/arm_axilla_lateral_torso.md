# Upper arm, axilla and side of torso: cited normals, healthy young adult women (for mesh checks)

Compiled 2026-10-19 from open abstracts/full texts (Europe PMC, PMC, publisher pages); no files downloaded. Confidence: strong = several independent sources agree; single = one study; weak = abstract/snippet only, indirect, secondary source, or my own derivation. "(calc)" = my arithmetic on cited numbers, not in the source. Mean +/- SD unless "P5/P50/P95". All values women unless said.

## 0. How this region is built and how it behaves at rest (my words, textbook anatomy; not re-verified this session)
1. Under the skin the arm is a bone-centred stack: humerus in the middle, flexors (biceps over brachialis, with coracobrachialis high and medial) in front, triceps behind, then a fat layer, then thin skin. Fat is thickest behind (triceps) and thinner in front and on the inner side.
2. At the top the arm root is cradled by two muscle ridges: pectoralis major in front (the anterior axillary fold) and latissimus dorsi plus teres major behind (the posterior fold). The two ridges are tied together at the floor by the axillary fascia.
3. The armpit hollow is a skin dimple held down by the suspensory ligament of the axilla, a fascial sheet running from the clavipectoral fascia below pectoralis minor to the axillary fascia and skin. It is why the hollow deepens with the arm raised and fills in with the arm down.
4. The side of the chest is ribs, then a thin sheet of serratus anterior (with latissimus behind it), then a thin fat layer, then skin. It is rib-supported, flat to gently convex (my reading; no curvature data found); the soft bulk of the region is at the folds, not on the ribs.
5. With the arm hanging, the shoulder blade is rotated slightly up and the glenohumeral joint is only about 5 degrees abducted (CT), so the upper arm hangs roughly 10 degrees off vertical (my inference, section 3) and almost alongside the ribs. Contact is at the fold and along the fat of the inner arm; no study measures the squash.
6. Inference, not measured: bone and muscle lie under the fat, so only skin plus fat (about 8 to 12 mm at mid-arm in the ultrasound data) can compress; the arm at rest cannot flatten into the trunk by centimetres.
7. The inner (medial) arm surface is the thinnest-skinned, thinnest-fat side: skin about 1 mm, fat about 7 mm at mid-arm, over biceps/brachialis and the neurovascular groove. I found no measurement of how flat this side is.
8. The axillary fold sits roughly 9 to 10 cm below the shoulder tip in standing women, about a third of the way down the upper arm.
9. Torso cross-sections at breast level are ellipses about 1.5 to 1.7 times wider than deep (bone cage alone about 1.55); the arm root attaches on the long-axis end.
10. Direct numbers for medial-arm flatness, gap, contact area, fold geometry and hollow depth do not exist in the sources I could reach (section 6). Rules below are built from layer thicknesses and anthropometry.

## 6. Gaps, stated plainly
- No measured outline ellipse ratio or medial flatness for the young-woman upper arm; the only hint is indirect (Heymsfield).
- No measurement of the arm-to-torso gap, contact area or soft-tissue squash under resting weight; Yoshida gives joint angles only.
- No numbers for pectoralis major, latissimus, teres major, triceps or coracobrachialis thickness in healthy young women; serratus and biceps only.
- No axillary fat pad thickness or volume, fold-to-fold distance, hollow depth, or suspensory ligament dimensions.
- ANSUR II percentiles were taken from a secondary explorer (read 2026-10-19) of the public file; SDs not shown. Approximate SD = (P95-P5)/3.29 (calc): axilla height 5.6, acromion-axilla 1.1, chest breadth 1.8, chest depth 2.8, flexed biceps 3.1 cm. For primary stats open the ANSUR II female CSV (do not mix with the 1988 ANSUR).
- Ultrasound fat (BodyMetrix, portable non-medical device) reads lower than caliper double-fold at triceps, biceps, subscapular, supra-iliac and higher at abdomen (Lewandowski); use the ultrasound figures for layer thickness.

## 7. How to use for a mesh check (suggested)
Proposed checks (my rules, not cited targets except where numbers are given): slice at 25/50/75% of acromion-radiale length (31.1 cm) and at the fold (9.5 cm below acromion); report circumference and width/depth. No source gives a flatness limit or ratio for the arm, so test the inner side for convexity (curvature sign) wherever skin + fat + flexor is about 25-29 mm over a humerus centred near mid-depth; a flat or concave medial wall at rest is unsupported. Shell: skin about 1 mm inner, 1.5 mm outer; fat 7-10 mm. Chest slice at bust height: width/depth 1.55-1.7 for bare torso outline, about 1.1 with bust. Fold: 7.8-11.5 cm below acromion (P5-P95).
