# Hand and wrist: cited normals for adults, for 3-D mesh checks

Compiled 2026-10-19 from abstracts, open full texts (Europe PMC) and one public percentile explorer; no files kept. The WebSearch budget ran out mid-session, so some sub-topics are thin (section 9). Confidence: strong = several independent sources agree; single = one study; weak = abstract/snippet only, secondary, small n, or my derivation. "(calc)" = my arithmetic on cited numbers. Mean +/- SD unless P5/P50/P95. Millimetres unless "cm". Keys [..] are in section 10.

## 0. How the hand is built and behaves (my words, textbook anatomy; not re-verified this session)
1. Bone frame: 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals (M2 longest, M5 and M1 shortest), 14 phalanges (thumb 2, fingers 3). Each finger is three segments; by joint-centre length the proximal one is about half, the middle about 0.26-0.29, the distal about 0.21-0.25 (calc, D12).
2. Joints: MCP (flexion/extension plus a few degrees to ~25 of spread), PIP and DIP (hinges), thumb CMC (saddle; two non-intersecting, non-perpendicular axes [HOL]). Wrist: radiocarpal + midcarpal, flexion/extension and radial/ulnar deviation.
3. Palm layers: thick epidermis, dermis, fat chambered by fibrous septa into pads (thenar, hypothenar, MCP pads, digital pulps), palmar aponeurosis. Dorsum: thin loose skin, almost no fat, extensor tendons and the dorsal vein network lie directly under it.
4. Creases are skin folds placed near, not on, the joint lines (section 5). Fingers: proximal, middle, distal digital creases; palm: radial longitudinal (thenar), proximal transverse, distal transverse; wrist: proximal and distal creases.
5. Dorsal skin has to lengthen over a flexed MCP/PIP (kinematic bound in D20); the surplus at rest sits as transverse wrinkles over PIP/DIP and skin is taut over the knuckles in a fist. Rest wrinkle counts and strains: not found.
6. In a fist the MC heads form the dorsal knuckle row, the extensor tendons slide distally, dorsal veins are stretched flat; the palm cups, the thenar and hypothenar pads bulge and the digital pulps flatten against the object (qualitative; numbers in section 8).
7. Sex: men larger in every length (+7 to +14% at P50, calc), 2D:4D lower, ridge density lower (coarser ridges), epidermis thicker, nails wider. Age: ridge density falls as the hand widens [SAN]. Build: hand surface as % of body surface falls as BMI rises [RHO].
8. Landmark families matter: digit length measured from the palmar proximal crease is ~20-27 mm shorter than from the dorsal MCP knuckle (calc, section 1). Always state the landmark.

## 0c. How to measure on a 3-D mesh
- Landmarks: W = midpoint of distal wrist crease; D1..D5 = digit tips; P1..P5 = midpoint of proximal palmar digital crease; MR2/MU5 = most radial point of MC2 head, most ulnar of MC5 head; joint centres from the skeleton (centre of curvature of the proximal bone's head).
- Hand length = |W-D3| projected on the hand axis; palm length = |W-P3|; breadth = |MR2-MU5| in the palm plane; hand index = breadth/length. Digit length = |Pi-Di| (palmar) and report the landmark. 2D:4D = |P2-D2| / |P4-D4| on the palmar side.
- Joint widths and circumferences: planar sections normal to the phalanx axis at the DIP and PIP joint lines (not the creases); breadth = max extent in the palm plane; circle-equivalent diameter = C/pi (tape reads ~10-13% above calliper breadth, calc from [URM]).
- Hand thickness: stadium model t = (C - 2w)/(pi - 2) from circumference C and breadth w gives F 27 mm, M 32 mm (calc; Iran knuckle depth 26 / 27 mm [URM] agrees at F).
- Web angle: angle between radial index border and ulnar thumb border at the web apex with the thumb extended in the palm plane. Surface area: sum of triangle areas, palmar side only; compare 0.87% of body surface.
- Nail: outline (width = max lateral extent of the plate, length = proximal fold to free edge), transverse curvature from a cross-section; plate thickness only if the mesh carries a shell.
- Ridges: if the fingertip has relief, FFT of the height field gives pitch; compare with D17 (pitch 0.4-0.55 mm, calc, section 5).
- Fist and grip: joint angles on the rig; check MC-head row curvature and crease positions against sections 3 and 5.
