**Orbit and eye** [A] Yuwatanakorn et al. 2021, *Vet World* 14:727 (CT, DSH vs brachycephalic) - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8076462/
- **Orbital diameter.** 26.5 ± 1.7 mm (DSH); 27.0 mm in brachycephalic cats.
- **Orbital depth.** 31.8-32.1 mm (DSH); 30.1-30.3 mm in brachycephalic cats.
- **Globe.** Axial 20.2 ± 0.3 mm, width about 19.8 mm, height about 20.2 mm. The eyeball is close to a sphere of about 20 mm.
- **How much of the eye sticks out.** 9-10 % of the eyeball lies outside the bony orbit in DSH, about 40 % in Persians. [A] Sieslack et al. 2021, *PLoS One* 16:e0254420 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8294563
- **Orbit convergence.** 65.4° ± 5.8° (n = 6) with a binocular field of 120°. [B, unverified] Heesy 2004, *Anat Rec* 281A:1104 - https://anatomypubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ar.a.20116
  - A different angle convention gives *F. silvestris* convergence 33.2° and frontation 79.0°. Do not mix the two conventions. [S] Finarelli 2009 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2707091/
- **Postorbital bar.** Incomplete: the frontal and jugal processes approach but do not meet. [S] Mivart 1881 - https://archive.org/stream/catintroduction00miva/catintroduction00miva_djvu.txt
  - Reighard & Jennings name the "zygomatic (or postorbital) process" of the frontal but do not state whether the bar is closed. [A]

- **Brachycephalic skulls.**

  - The face and upper canines rotate upward. [S] Schlueter et al. 2009, *J Feline Med Surg* - https://bib.vetmed.fu-berlin.de/pubdb/pub/133-brachycephalic-feline-noses-ct-and-anatomical-study-of-the-rela
- **Mandible and jaw angle.**

  - No modern value for the mandibular (gonial) angle was found.

  - Head length exceeds skull length by about 15-20 mm (derived). The two samples are different populations, so treat this soft-tissue allowance as rough.

### Face soft tissue

**Whisker (mystacial) pads and lips**
- **Whisker pad muscle.** M. quadratus labii superioris attaches mainly to the whisker pad. [A] Reighard & Jennings, *Anatomy of the Cat* (1901), Muscles of the Head - https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/58394/pg58394-images.html
- **Lip and face muscles.** The orbicularis oris is "a very thin layer of subcutaneous concentric fibres". The zygomaticus major is a "slender band" from the corner of the mouth to the ear's scutiform cartilage. The platysma forms "a thin layer of fibres covering the sides of the neck and face" and inserts into the lips and eyelids. [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901
- **Cat lips are small.** Feline lips are smaller and less mobile than canine lips. [B] WikiVet, Lips - https://en.wikivet.net/Special:MobileDiff/15607...208195
- **Philtrum.** Dog and cat have a median groove, the philtrum, in the upper lip. [B] University of Diyala vet anatomy lecture, Digestive 2 - https://vetmed.uodiyala.edu.iq/uploads/2019/dr%20khaled%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AD/digestive%202.pdf
- **Upper lip versus lower lip (closed mouth).**
  - The upper lip is crossed at the midline by the philtrum.
  - The upper lip "is always larger than the lower lip, which it overlaps or covers to a greater or lesser extent". The size difference is "significant in … carnivores".
  - [A] IMAIOS vet-Anatomy, Upper lip (cites Barone, *Anatomie comparée des mammifères domestiques* vol. 3, 2017) - https://www.imaios.cn/en/vet-anatomy/anatomical-structures/upper-lip-11073761600
  - **For the build:** with the mouth closed, the upper lip and whisker pad hang over and partly hide the smaller lower lip.
- **Subnasal groove.** On imaging, a subnasal groove is visible just below the external nose in the cat. [A] Díaz Martínez et al. 2024, *Animals* 14:1172 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11047709
- **NOT FOUND.** No cat-specific measurement of:
  - the overhang;
  - whether the upper lip hides the lower canine;
  - the pigmented lip margin.

**Nose leather (planum nasale) and nostrils**
- **Naked skin.** The end of the nose is naked. [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901, Integument - https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/58394/pg58394.txt; Mivart 1881 - https://archive.org/stream/catanintroducti00mivagoog/catanintroducti00mivagoog_djvu.txt
- **Structure.**
  - The planum is the most rostral part of the nose, pigmented and hairless.
  - It is supported by three pairs of cartilages (dorsolateral, ventrolateral, accessory) joined by ligaments.
  - The septal cartilage divides it at the midline.
  - [B] IVIS, SEVC 2017, "How I treat… carcinoma of nasal planum in cats" - https://www.ivis.org/library/sevc/sevc-annual-conference-barcelona-2017/how-i-treat%E2%80%A6-carcinoma-of-nasal-planum-cats
- **Nostrils.** The domestic cat's nostril opening is narrow, like the lion's and unlike the leopard's or cheetah's. Nostril width depends on the alar fold. In the cat the alar fold is more vertical and elongated and ends more dorsally. The nasal wing and alar fold are proportionally deeper than in big cats. [A] Díaz Martínez et al. 2024 (URL above)
- **Planum outline.** On rhinoscopy, the apex of the planum is "more elongated" in the cat than in the cheetah, which is more rectangular. [A] Díaz Martínez et al. 2024

**Eyelids and third eyelid**
- **Lid surfaces.** Externally covered with hair; internally lined by conjunctiva. [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901, Eye
- **Meibomian glands.** Arranged "in short rows passing perpendicularly inward from the edge of the lids". [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901
- **Lashes.**
  - Upper lid: no true cilia; the first row of skin hairs does the job.
  - Lower lid: no cilia.
  - [A] Dechra, Anatomy of the Eye: Upper and Lower Lids - https://digital.dechra.com/Anatomy%20of%20the%20eye%20MASTER%20-%20FINAL/Anatomy%20of%20the%20eye%20V2/sections/Upper_Lower_Lids.html
- **Third eyelid: size and support.** The nictitating membrane "in the cat … is large and may cover the whole surface of the eye". It is stiffened by "a broad central strip of cartilage". The large Harderian glands are associated with it. [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901
- **Third eyelid: position and movement.** It lies in the conjunctival sac at the medial canthus. Its cartilage is T-shaped. It moves across the eye passively when the globe is retracted. [A] Dechra, Third Eyelid and Gland - https://digital.dechra.com/Anatomy%20of%20the%20eye%20MASTER%20-%20FINAL/Anatomy%20of%20the%20eye%20V2/sections/Third_Eyelid_and_gland.html
- **Third eyelid: colour.** It is somewhat translucent. [C] Scientific American - https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-mystery-of-the-cat-s-inner-eyelid
- **Rest position.** No source gives how much third eyelid shows at rest, or its edge pigmentation.

**Ear pinna, cartilage, folds and Henry's pocket**
- **Three cartilages.** The external ear has three elastic cartilages: annular, scutiform and auricular. The auricular cartilage expands to form the pinna. The scutiform cartilage is L-shaped and lies over the temporalis, inside the auricular muscles. [A] Kumar & Roman-Auerhahn, "Anatomy of the Canine and Feline Ear" - https://veteriankey.com/anatomy-of-the-canine-and-feline-ear/
- **Carriage and muscles.** The pinna is mostly upright in the cat. It is moved by three sets of muscles (rostral, ventral, caudal), all supplied by the facial nerve. [A] Kumar & Roman-Auerhahn
- **Pinna shape.**
  - The pinna is "a thin curved plate of cartilage".
  - "A deep furrow, corresponding to the antihelix, separates scapha from concha externally."
  - The tragus "projects proximad as a blunt point".
  - The inner surface is "marked by a number of very prominent ridges and projections".
  - [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901, Ear
- **Orientation and hair.** The cavity faces "forwards and outwards". There are long hairs inside the ear and sometimes on its tip. [A] Mivart 1881
- **Ear canal.** Devoid of hairs. [A] Kumar & Roman-Auerhahn
- **Henry's pocket (cutaneous marginal pouch, saccus cutaneus marginalis).**
  - "A cutaneous fold forming a small pouch on the antitragic border in the carnivore; only its rostral wall is supported by cartilage". [A] IMAIOS vet-Anatomy, citing Constantinescu & Schaller, *Illustrated Veterinary Anatomical Nomenclature* (2012) - https://www.imaios.cn/en/vet-anatomy/anatomical-structures/cutaneous-marginal-pouch-11107451932
  - Its function is unproven. [C] Wikipedia, Henry's pocket - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry%27s_pocket
  - **For the build:** the pouch is a skin fold at the lower outer (lateral) edge of the pinna, on the antitragic border. Its rostral wall is backed by cartilage and its caudal wall is skin only.

## (b) Superficial muscles that shape the surface
Source for every item: [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901, Muscles - https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/58394/pg58394-images.html (offsets 200k-500k). Quotes are theirs.
Cat names differ from dog and horse texts. There is no single "brachiocephalicus" in Reighard & Jennings. The cat has a clavicle, and the equivalent sheet is the clavotrapezius (skull and neck to the clavicle) continued by the clavobrachialis (clavicle to the ulna).

### Skin muscles (they move the skin, not the shape)
- **Cutaneus maximus.** "A very large, thin muscle" covering "almost the whole side of the body".
- **Platysma.** A thin sheet over the neck and face.

### Head
| Muscle | Attachments | Visible form |
|---|---|---|
| Temporalis | Temporal fossa to the coronoid process | "the great mass" in the temporal fossa. Bulk over the side and top of the cranium |
| Masseter | Malar (zygomatic) bone to the mandible | "the projecting mass so prominent in the cat behind and below the eye". The cheek bulge |
| Digastric | Jugular process and mastoid to the ventral mandible border | "a thick prismatic muscle". Under the jaw |

### Trunk
- **External oblique.** A "large, thin sheet" over the abdomen.
- **Rectus abdominis.** "Rather thick, flat"; about 4 cm wide at the first lumbar vertebra.
- **Longissimus dorsi.** "A very large muscle" filling the space along the lumbar and thoracic spines. It is the epaxial ridge either side of the spine.

| Semitendinosus | Ischial tuberosity to the tibial crest | "long slender"; back of the thigh |

| Gracilis | Pubic symphysis to the tibia | "flat, thin, subcutaneous", inner thigh |
| Vastus lateralis / rectus femoris | Femur and ilium to the patella | Vastus lateralis covers the upper (dorsal) part of the outer thigh |

### Tail
- **Extensor caudae medialis and lateralis.** Run along the top (dorsal) of the tail; the lateralis "grows continually smaller" toward the tip.
- **Abductor caudae externus.** A "rounded muscle" on the side of the tail, to about caudal vertebra 8-9.
- **Flexor caudae longus.** "Long, strong tendons" on the underside.

### Counts
**Vertebrae**
- 7 cervical, 13 thoracic, 7 lumbar, 3 sacral (fused). [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901 - https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/58394/pg58394-images.html; [A] Mivart 1881 (sacral "three (sometimes four)") - https://archive.org/stream/catanintroducti00mivagoog/catanintroducti00mivagoog_djvu.txt
- **Caudal count: sources disagree.**
  - Reighard & Jennings: "twenty-two or twenty-three", and elsewhere 21-23.
  - Mivart: "usually about eighteen or nineteen … sometimes as many as twenty-four".
  - Use 18-23.
- **Spinous process direction.** The tenth thoracic spine is vertical (the anticlinal vertebra). T11-T13 point cranially. [A] Reighard & Jennings
  - T1-T9 slope backward and the lumbar spines point forward. [S] Reighard & Jennings

**Ribs and sternum**
- **Ribs.** 13 pairs. [A] Reighard & Jennings; [A] Mivart
  - Ribs 1-9 are true ribs, each joining the sternum by its own cartilage.
  - Ribs 10-13 are false ribs: the cartilages of 10-12 unite and join the 9th cartilage or the sternum, or are free.
  - Rib 13 floats.
  - In short: 9 sternal + 3 asternal on the costal arch + 1 floating.
- **Sternum.** Manubrium + 6 body segments + xiphoid = 8 sternebrae. [A] Reighard & Jennings; [A] Mivart (about eight)

**Clavicle**
- "Greatly reduced … a slender curved rod of bone imbedded in the muscles of the shoulder and connected by fibrous tissue to the apex of the acromion process". [A] Reighard & Jennings
- About 2-5 cm long. [S] IMAIOS (citing König & Liebich) - https://www.imaios.cn/en/vet-anatomy/anatomical-structures/clavicle-11073897868

**Feet**
- **Front foot.** The pollex has 2 phalanges; the other digits have 3. [A] Reighard & Jennings
- **Hind foot.** 4 digits plus a rudimentary first metatarsal; 7 tarsal bones. [S] Reighard & Jennings
- **Pads.** One large pad per foot, a small pad for each digit, and on the forelimb a small conical (carpal) pad. [A] Reighard & Jennings, Integument. That makes 7 pads on the fore paw and 5 on the hind paw. [A] Mivart 1881

### Posture
- **Digitigrade stance.** The cat stands on its digits. The only source found stating this outright is tertiary. [C] Wikipedia, Cat
  - Day & Jayne measure the metacarpophalangeal and metatarsophalangeal joints as working joints in stance, which fits standing on the toes. [A]
- **Joint angles in walking cats** (180° = straight; footfall → midstance). [A] Day & Jayne 2007
  - Elbow 129 → 127°
  - Wrist 184 → 176°
  - Metacarpophalangeal 133 → 107°
  - Knee 130 → 115°
  - Ankle 118 → 114°
  - Metatarsophalangeal 140 → 112°
  - Shoulder height is 74 % of forelimb length and hip height 77 % of hindlimb length at midstance (a crouched posture).
  - No values for a cat standing still were found.
- **Segment angles from vertical at midstance.** Humerus −45°, radius 8°, metacarpal 11°, femur 13°, tibia −51°, metatarsal 13°. [S] Day & Jayne 2007
- **Joint range of motion** (100 cats; usable as rig limits). Shoulder about 135°, elbow about 144°, carpus about 185°, hip about 122°, stifle about 147°, tarsus about 148°. [S] Lascelles et al. 2012 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3293090/
- **Spine in a standing cat** (not one simple arch). [S] Macpherson & Ye 1998, *Exp Brain Res* 119:324 (abstract via Europe PMC) - https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22cat%20vertebral%20column%22%20AND%20AUTH:%22Macpherson%22&format=json&resultType=core
  - Lower lumbar region bends mildly upward (dorsiflexed).
  - Lower thoracic to upper lumbar region bends clearly downward (ventroflexed).
  - Upper thoracic region (above T9) and the neck bend strongly upward.
  - The shoulder joint sits just below the bodies of T3-T5.
  - The top (dorsal border) of the scapula rises above the thoracic spines.
- **Neck at rest.** The cervical column is near-vertical, with the S-curve turning at C7/T1. [S] Vidal et al. 1986 - https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF00237580
- **Trunk and limbs in stance.** The trunk is held horizontal. The limbs pivot at the girdles while the geometry within each limb stays constant. [S] Fung & Macpherson 1995 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6577830
- **Ribcage.** A "long, transversely narrow, conical case", deeper than wide, so laterally compressed rather than barrel-shaped. [S] Mivart 1881. The thorax gets shallower toward the front. [A] Reighard & Jennings

## Gaps / uncertain
1. **Skull measurements are incomplete.** No primary mean ± SD was found for condylobasal length, facial length, nasal bone length or zygomatic width; Künzel 2003 and the Schmidt supplementary tables were not reachable. Zygomatic width and facial length above are derived or come from museum lists.
2. **The orbit convergence angle (65.4°, Heesy 2004) is unverified.** No modern value for the mandibular (gonial) angle was found.
3. **Closed-mouth lip geometry is unmeasured.** No cat-specific data on overhang, the line where the lips meet, lip-margin pigment, or how much third eyelid shows at rest.
4. **Henry's pocket has only an anatomical definition.** Antitragic border, rostral wall cartilage-backed; there are no dimensions.
5. **Several bone lengths are missing.** No absolute lengths for MC III, MT III, phalanges, fibula or clavicle (except the IMAIOS 2-5 cm range). Tail length comes only from tertiary sources.
6. **No joint angles for a cat standing still.** The walking midstance values are proxies, and shoulder and hip angles were not reported.
7. **Spine-curvature and girdle descriptions are abstract-level only** (Macpherson & Ye 1998; Fung & Macpherson 1995).
8. **The muscle notes describe position and size in words only.** They come from a 1901 dissection text, with no muscle volumes or thicknesses. There is no open volumetric cat-muscle 3-D data.
9. **3-D data licences.** The best whole-skeleton scan (3DAS:00001) is non-commercial and needs a MorphoSource account. Only the ELTE skulls are CC0, and their one-file 51 GB zip would need the per-file ELTE mirror. The Sketchfab skulls are CC BY but carry no unit or scale information.
10. **Items tagged [S] or [B] were not re-opened by the author of this file.**
