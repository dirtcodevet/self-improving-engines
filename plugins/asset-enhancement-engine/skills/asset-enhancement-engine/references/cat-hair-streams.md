# Cat hair: streams, types, density, pigment (research notes)

Compiled 2026-10-17 from web pages only. Nothing was downloaded on purpose.
Every claim has a source. Confidence tags:
- **[A]** read on the cited page (primary paper, vet text, breed standard).
- **[B]** read in a search-result summary of that source. The page itself was blocked, a PDF, or not opened.
- **[C]** popular or low-quality source. Use only as a hint.

Direction words: "caudal" means toward the tail, "rostral" toward the nose tip. Kidd's "proximal" along the muzzle means from the nose tip toward the head.

## (a) Hair streams, whorls, partings, convergence lines

### Terms
- **Hair slope (Haarstrich).** Hair leaves the skin at an oblique, directed angle, which gives the coat its stroke direction. Where regions with different directions meet, they form crests (Kämme), partings (Scheitel) or whorls (Haarwirbel). In most mammals the tips point backwards. [A] Spektrum Lexikon der Biologie, "Haarstrich" - https://www.spektrum.de/lexikon/biologie/haarstrich/30064
- **Cat hairs are set obliquely but can be raised.** The tail hair stands on end when the cat is enraged. [A] Mivart, *The Cat* (1881), ch. II section 13 - https://archive.org/stream/catanintroducti00mivagoog/catanintroducti00mivagoog_djvu.txt
- **Where whorls form.** Kidd: whorls and featherings mark where hair changes from the normal backward slope. They are common in short-haired mammals and absent or rudimentary in long-haired ones. [B] Nature review of Kidd, *Use-Inheritance* (1901) - https://www.nature.com/articles/065004a0
- **Whorls in a long-haired build.** Expect any whorl to be weaker or hidden on a long-haired cat. This follows from the point above.

### Whole-body baseline for the domestic cat
- **Overall direction.** "The hairs are directed backwards from the head to the tail, and, for the most part, downwards on the limbs." [A] Mivart 1881, ch. II section 1 (URL above)
- **Hair length by region.** Hair is always short on the paws and face, and often longer on the belly and tail. [A] Mivart 1881, ch. II section 1
- **Naked skin.** The nose tip, the lips and the paw pads are naked. [A] Mivart 1881, ch. II section 1
- **Naked skin, second source.** Only the end of the nose, the foot pads and the nipples lack hair. [A] Reighard & Jennings, *Anatomy of the Cat* (1901), Integument - https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/58394/pg58394.txt
- **Primitive slope.** Kidd treats the primitive mammalian slope as running "from the tip of the snout to the caudal extremity of the animal, along the head, neck and trunk." [A] Kidd, *The Direction of Hair in Animals and Man* (1903) - https://archive.org/stream/cu31924003075425/cu31924003075425_djvu.txt

### Head and face

**Nasal bridge: the one cat-specific feature with a source.**
- **Ordinary carnivore type.** In dogs, foxes, wolves, jackals, "Smaller Cats" and most bears, hair on the muzzle slopes from the nose tip back toward the forehead. This is the "Ordinary Type" on the nasal and frontal regions. It starts from a small whorl or star just above the muzzle. [A] Kidd 1900, "The Significance of the Hair-Slope in certain Mammals", *Proc. Zool. Soc. London* 1900: 676-686 - https://archive.org/stream/biostor-107788/biostor-107788_djvu.txt
- **Domestic cat is an exception.** Kidd names the Domestic Cat with lion, tiger, leopard, jaguar, puma, lynx and cheetah. In these cats "the direction of the slope of hair on the nasal region is towards the external nares." [A] Kidd 1900 (URL above)
- **Where the reversal starts: two versions from the same author.**
  - 1900: a whorl "situated about midway between the level of the orbits and the external nares". From it the hair runs straight to the muzzle. [A] Kidd 1900 (URL above)
  - 1903: "At the level of the orbits there commences by a central small crest a distal direction of reversed hair on the muzzle which passes to the tip". [A] Kidd 1903 (URL above)
  - So the start is either a whorl midway down the nose or a small crest at eye level. The sources disagree and I found no modern check.
- **How to see it.** On a black domestic cat in some lights, this patch shows as a differently shaded area that stands out from the rest. [A] Kidd 1903
- **"Smaller Cats" versus Domestic Cat.** Kidd 1900 puts "Smaller Cats" (small wild felids) in the ordinary group but names the Domestic Cat as an exception. He explains the split by the deeper, more massive muzzle. This is his claim; I found nothing measuring it.
- **What this means for the build (inference from the above).** Forehead and crown hair slopes back toward the neck. Nasal-bridge hair, from about eye level (or mid-nose) down to the nose leather, slopes toward the nostrils. The two meet in a crest or whorl on the midline.

**Around the eyes.**
- **Upper lid.** Cats have no true cilia (eyelashes). "Instead the first row of skin hairs are adapted for the same purpose." [A] Dechra Veterinary Products, Anatomy of the Eye: Upper and Lower Lids - https://digital.dechra.com/Anatomy%20of%20the%20eye%20MASTER%20-%20FINAL/Anatomy%20of%20the%20eye%20V2/sections/Upper_Lower_Lids.html
- **Lower lid.** "Neither the dog nor the cat have cilia on the bottom lid." [A] Dechra (same page)
- **Eyebrow hairs.** "There are also a few long hairs over each eye, or eyebrows, but there are no eyelashes." [A] Mivart 1881, ch. II section 1
- **Lid skin.** The eyelids are covered with hair on the outside and lined by conjunctiva on the inside. [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901, Eye (txt URL above)
- **General mammal note.** Kidd sees the orbit as a place where lashes are "set nearly at a right angle" to the surface, unlike the usual oblique hair. [A] Kidd 1903
- **Abnormal case.** Trichiasis (normal-site periocular hair pointing at the eye) is rare in cats. When it occurs it is "usually bilateral at lateral aspect of upper lids". Facial-fold trichiasis is common in brachycephalic cats. [B] 5-Minute Veterinary Consult (feline) via Skyscape - https://download.skyscape.com/download/ota/lonestar/E4105FE0-F4DD-4EF7-963E-12E351DF1C6F/fmvetcf.ed6/content/HTML/c/tu4.htm
- **NOT FOUND.** No veterinary, scientific or museum source I could reach gives the hair direction:
  - above the eye or below the eye;
  - at the inner (medial) or outer (lateral) canthus;
  - on the temple between eye and ear;
  - on the cheek or on the whisker pad.
- **What was searched.** English and German terms: hair streams, Haarströme, Haarstrich, flumina pilorum, vortices pilorum, hair whorl cat, periocular hair direction. Also the books Kidd 1900/1903, Mivart 1881 and Reighard & Jennings 1901.
- **What was rejected.** Art tutorials describe fur "radiating from the nose" with a "circular pattern around the eyes" ([C] https://library.peoplespharmacy.com/draw-cat-face). They are not anatomy sources and are not used.
- **Do not use tabby lines as a direction map.** Tabby facial markings are pigment patterns, and no source links them to hair direction. Examples: the "M" on the forehead, the "unbroken line runs back from outer corner of eye", "swirls on cheeks". [A] CFA Persian standard - https://cfa.org/breed/persian/

**Chin, throat, cheeks and whisker pads.** No cat-specific direction source found. Only the general rule applies: backwards from head to tail (Mivart). The whisker pad carries the mystacial tactile hairs; see (b).

### Ear
- **Concha and hairs.** The deep cavity of each ear is "directed forwards and outwards". "There are long hairs inside each ear and sometimes on its tip." [A] Mivart 1881, ch. II section 1
- **Ear canal.** "The external ear canal of the cat is devoid of hairs." [A] Kumar & Roman-Auerhahn, "Anatomy of the Canine and Feline Ear" (Veterian Key, 2016) - https://veteriankey.com/anatomy-of-the-canine-and-feline-ear/
- **Breed standards.** CFA standards refer to "ear tufts" as a distinct coat area; for chinchilla Persians they stay white. [A] CFA Persian standard (URL above)
- **NOT FOUND.** No source gives hair direction on the ear back (convex side), the rim, or the inner pinna.

### Neck, chest, forelimb, trunk, hindlimb, tail
- **Body and limbs.** Backwards from head to tail, and mostly downwards on the limbs. [A] Mivart 1881
- **Pectoral whorl.** Kidd describes a pectoral whorl as "found in most Ungulates and Carnivores". He does not name the cat. [A] Kidd 1900
- **Forearm.** Reversed hair slope on the extensor (dorsal) surface of the forearm (ulna) "is found in nearly all Carnivores, wild and domesticated". Kidd ties it to resting with the forelimbs planted forward. He does not name the cat. [A] Kidd 1900. On the flexor surface "in all animals it follows the ordinary slope." [A] Kidd 1903
- **Big cats only.**
  - Lion, tiger and leopard have a whorl with forward feathering and a crest at the root of the neck on each side. [A] Kidd 1903
  - A dorsal whorl and crest is "peculiar to the Lion, and is found in no others of the FELIDÆ." [A] Kidd 1903
  - Neither applies to the domestic cat.
- **Dog analogue only (not cat data).** In 120 dogs, whorls usually occurred on the chest, the brachial axillary region, the elbows and the ischiatic region (back of the thigh). Whorls on the head, neck, shoulders, thoracic axilla and ventral abdomen occurred in fewer than 20 % of dogs. [A] Tomkins & McGreevy 2010, "Hair whorls in the dog. I. Distribution", *Anat Rec* 293:338-350 - https://wellbeingintlstudiesrepository.org/anatom/9
- **Dog left-right rule.** Bilateral whorls rotate in opposite directions, so the coat stays symmetrical. [B] Tomkins & McGreevy 2010, Part II - https://www.wellbeingintlstudiesrepository.org/anatom/8
- **Tail.** Hair is longer here and follows the backward rule. [A] Mivart 1881. No tail crest or parting is described for cats.

## (b) Hair types, sizes and grouping

### The three coat hair types
- **Definitions.** "The fur of a common cat consists of three hair types: long and straight guard hair of uniform diameter," "thinner awn hairs, and fine undulating down hair of uniform thickness." [A] Gandolfi & Lyons, "Cornish Rex Unraveled", PAG XX (2012) - https://pag.confex.com/pag/xx/webprogram/Paper3118.html
- **Same wording in a paper.** [A] Gandolfi et al. 2013, *Sci Rep* 3:2000 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3683669
- **Rex coats.** The Devon Rex coat "lacks the guard hair and shows a reduction in length and thickness of the remaining fibers". The Selkirk Rex has "tightly curled hair shafts". [A] Gandolfi et al. 2013 (URL above)
- **Guard hairs** are "generally stiff and straight and form the outer haircoat". [A] WikiVet, Hair - Anatomy & Physiology - https://en.wikivet.net/Hair_-_Anatomy_&_Physiology
- **Wool (down) hairs** are "thin, wavy, soft hairs that form the undercoat". [A] WikiVet (same page)
- **Two layers and their ratio.** Fur has thick guard hairs on top and thin down hairs underneath. Down hairs "can outnumber guard hairs 24 to 1". [A] Noel & Hu 2018, "Cats use hollow papillae to wick saliva into fur", *PNAS* 115:12377 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6298077/

### Compound follicles (how hairs group)
- **Cats have compound follicles.** "There is a cluster of two to five primary hairs surrounded by groups of smaller secondary hairs". One central primary is the largest, with smaller lateral primaries around it. "Five to 20 secondary hairs may accompany each primary hair." [A] Moriello 2011, dvm360 Proceedings - https://dvm360.com/view/bald-not-beautiful-if-you-are-cat-managing-alopecia-cats-proceedings
- **Glands per hair.** Each primary hair has a sebaceous gland, an arrector pili muscle and a sweat gland. Secondary hairs may only have a sebaceous gland. [A] Moriello 2011
- **Shared exit.** Dogs and cats have simple follicles mixed with compound follicles. A compound follicle is one where several shafts exit one infundibulum. [B] Ohio State University, Veterinary Histology, ch. 7 - https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/vethisto/chapter/7-dermal-adnexa-follicles/
- **Measured group size, normal DSH cats.**
  - 23.4 ± 5.4 follicles per follicle group.
  - Median 18.8 hairs per group (range 10.6-26.6).
  - [A] LeRoy et al. 2016
- **How a group develops.** Primary hairs form first. The first secondary hairs develop on either side of them, then bud further secondaries that complete the compound follicle. [B] Baker 1974 (URL above)
- **Secondary-to-primary (S/P) ratio.** About 3:1 in young cats, rising to 10-15:1 in mature cats. Lateral primaries are hard to tell from secondaries. [B] Ryder 1976 PDF - https://gwern.net/doc/cat/biology/1976-ryder.pdf

### Tactile and specialised hairs
- **Tylotrich hairs** are large follicles that act as rapidly adapting mechanoreceptors, scattered anywhere on the body. [A] WikiVet (URL above); Moriello 2011
- **Sinus (tactile) hairs** have a venous sinus and mechanoreceptor nerve endings. They are much thicker and stiffer than other hairs and sit mostly on the face, near the eyes and the upper lip. Cats have "a cluster on the carpus". [A] WikiVet
- **Where the cat's vibrissae are.** "A number of long stiff sensory hairs (vibrissæ) are found on the upper lip, the cheek, and above the upper eyelid." [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901, Integument
- **Mystacial (upper lip) count.**
  - "About a dozen very long and strong hairs" on each upper lip. [A] Mivart 1881
  - 12 per side in 4 rows, with the top two rows able to move separately from the bottom two. [C] Cat Behavior Associates - https://catbehaviorassociates.com/your-cats-whiskers/
  - Count varies with breed. [C] Country Life - https://www.countrylife.co.uk/comment-opinion/curious-questions-why-do-cats-have-whiskers-239192
- **Mystacial layout.** Across species, macrovibrissae sit in regular rows and get longer from front (rostral) to back (caudal). [B] Brecht et al. 1997, as summarised in the Wikipedia "Whiskers" article - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskers
- **Superciliary (above the eye).** "A few long hairs over each eye". [A] Mivart 1881. No reliable count found.
- **Genal (cheek).** Present on the cheek. [A] Reighard & Jennings 1901. No reliable count or length found.
- **Chin (mandibular).** Whiskers beneath the chin are reported. [C] VetStreet - https://www.vetstreet.com/dr-marty-becker/how-dog-and-cat-whiskers-work
- **Carpal.** "4 to 6 vibrissae 20 to 30 mm long in cats", on the back (caudal side) of the distal forearm. They occur in rodents and carnivores, but not in dogs. [A] IMAIOS vet-Anatomy, Carpal tactile hair organ (cites Nilsson 1976) - https://www.imaios.cn/en/vet-anatomy/anatomical-structures/carpal-tactile-hair-organ-11111647564
- **Nerves.** Maxillary (whisker) sinus hairs are supplied by the infraorbital nerve and carpal sinus hairs by the ulnar nerve. [B] Gottschaldt, Iggo & Young 1973, *J Physiol* - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5037100
- **Whisker taper.** Cheek vibrissae of the domestic cat have base-to-tip radius ratios of 7, 10 and 13 (3 longest whiskers). [A] Williams & Kramer 2010, *PLoS One* 5:e8806 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2808387/

## (c) Density and visible locks or clumps
- **No hairs-per-cm² figure for cats.** None found in a reliable source.
  - A frequently repeated web figure (about 20,000 hairs per cm², "130,000 per square inch") has no study behind it and is **not used**. [C] https://gna.it.com/how-many-hairs-does-a-cat-have
  - LeRoy et al. 2016 (above) give hairs per follicle group but not groups per area.
- **Dorsal versus ventral density.** Denser on the back than on the belly and inner limbs, but this is documented in goats and buffalo, not cats. Treat as an assumption if used. [A] Sultan & Abulraheem 2020, *Basrah J Vet Res* 19:174 (goat) - https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/682f2b889d0aa4165340b1d2
- **Growth by mass.** Mid-side hair growth is 62 µg/cm²/day in winter and 289 µg/cm²/day in summer. Total is 32.7 g of hair per kg of body weight per year (39 DSH cats). [A] Hendriks, Tarttelin & Moughan 1997, *Comp Biochem Physiol* 116:29 - https://agris.fao.org/search/ar/records/65dea7137c7033e84beb4145
- **Shedding pattern.** Shedding is mosaic (patchy), not in waves as in rodents. [B] Baker 1974; [A] WikiVet
- **Wet fur clumps.** "Cat fur forms clumps when wetted". The model assumes wet, clumped hair reaches a minimum porosity of 0.093, against 0.97 when dry. Persian fur mats easily. [A] Noel & Hu 2018
- **Physics of wet clumping.** Wet flexible hairs coalesce pairwise into hierarchical bundles, set by capillary force against hair stiffness. [B] Bico et al. 2004, *Nature* 432:690 - https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v432y2004i7018d10.1038_432690a.html
- **Clumping in dry fur.** No source describes how dry cat fur forms visible locks.
  - **Modelling suggestion, not a sourced fact:** a natural clump unit is the follicle group. That is about 19 hairs (LeRoy 2016) sharing one infundibulum (Ohio State), with 1 guard per up to about 24 down hairs (Noel & Hu).

## (d) Pigment along the hair shaft

### Agouti banding and tabby
- **Agouti signal.** Agouti protein is "a paracrine signaling molecule released from dermal papillae" and acts on Mc1r. [A] Kaelin et al. 2012, *Science* 337:1536 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709578
- **Non-agouti (solid) cats.** Recessive non-agouti *a* is a 2-bp ASIP deletion that "specifies black coloration in domestic cats". [A] OMIA 000201-9685 - https://omia.org/OMIA000201/9685; Eizirik et al. 2003, *Curr Biol* 13:448
- **Ghost pattern.** In non-agouti cats the tabby stripes become a "ghost pattern … difficult or impossible to visualize". [A] Kaelin 2012
- **Tabby at the hair level.**
  - Light background: "individual hairs have extensive light bands". Cat hairs "frequently exhibit multiple pheomelanic bands".
  - Dark stripes: hairs "have little or no banding".
  - [A] Kaelin 2012
- **What sets the stripes.**
  - Taqpep lays down the pattern: mackerel has "narrow vertical stripes"; blotched has "wide whorls".
  - Edn3 mRNA is "~twofold higher in dark as compared to light areas" and is "restricted to the dermal papilla".
  - [A] Kaelin 2012
- **Ticked (Abyssinian) pattern.** Loss-of-function DKK4 variants cause it; the dark areas shrink and crowd together. DKK4 marks fetal skin that later grows dark-pigment follicles. [B] Kaelin, McGowan & Barsh 2021, *Nat Commun* 12:5127, via Stanford Medicine news - https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2021/09/cat-fur-color-patterns.html
- **Band order on a ticked hair (breed standards).**
  - "Two or three dark bands of ticking". "The extreme outer tip to be the darkest". Undercoat "clear and bright to the skin". [A] CFA Abyssinian standard - https://cfa.org/breed/abyssinian/
  - From skin to tip, the hair starts with a light band and ends with a dark band. [B] LOOF standards - https://loof.asso.fr/sites/default/download/05-standards-all-201201.pdf
- **Abyssinian face.** "Eyes accentuated by fine dark line, encircled by light colored area". [A] CFA Abyssinian
- **Classic tabby face and body.** Forehead "M"; an unbroken line back from the outer corner of the eye; cheek swirls; necklaces; leg bracelets; tail rings. [A] CFA Persian standard
- **Mackerel tabby face and body.** Same layout as classic, but with narrow pencilings and no cheek swirls listed. [A] CFA Persian standard

### Tipping (silver and smoke series; colour only on the outer part of the shaft)
CFA definitions. [A] CFA Persian standard - https://cfa.org/breed/persian/
- **Chinchilla silver.** "Undercoat pure white". The back, flanks, head and tail are tipped with black. Chin, chest, stomach and ear tufts stay white.
- **Shaded silver.** White undercoat with a "mantle of black tipping shading down from sides, face, and tail".
- **Black smoke.** "Undercoat white, deeply tipped with black". "In motion the white undercoat is clearly apparent."
- **Shell cameo.** White undercoat, lightly tipped with red.
- **Silver tabby.** Pale clear silver ground with a white undercoat.

Tip length as a share of the hair. [A] CFA Burmilla standard - https://cfa.org/breed/burmilla/
- **Chinchilla:** about one-eighth of the hair length.
- **Shaded:** about one-third.

### Colour-point (temperature-restricted pigment)
- **Genes.**
  - Siamese (*cs*) and Burmese (*cb*) are temperature-sensitive tyrosinase (TYR) alleles. [A] Imes et al. 2006, *Anim Genet* 37:175 - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1464423
  - Burmese: G227W. Siamese: G302R. [B] OMIA 000202-9685 - https://omia.org/OMIA000202/9685/
- **Where the points are.**
  - Pigment is restricted to the extremities: paws, tail, ears, face. The Burmese allele is less temperature-sensitive and puts more pigment over the body. [B] UC Davis VGL, Colorpoint Restriction - https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/colorpoint-restriction
  - Points form in "the cooler extremities, such as ears, paws, and tail". Kittens have not yet developed points. [A] Carr, Memorial Univ. Newfoundland - https://www.mun.ca/biology/scarr/Albinism_in_cats.html
  - Points are ears, legs, feet, tail and mask. "The ideal mask extends from above the eyes down through the chin and stretches beyond the eyes from side to side." [A] CFA Persian (Himalayan) standard
- **Colour depends on skin temperature while the hair grows.**
  - 1930s *J Heredity* study: cats were cooled during shedding, then the shoulder and back were shaved and warmed with vests. The cream coat darkened when cold and lightened again where warmed. [A] AGA blog (Journal of Heredity publisher), 2026 - https://blog.theaga.org/?p=2771
  - Case report: a sacral patch clipped in winter regrew dark, "comparable to the color present on the body extremities". A clipped abdomen regrew normal colour. [A] Dourado et al. 2021, *Braz J Vet Res Anim Sci* 58:e180388 - https://revistas.usp.br/bjvras/article/view/180388
  - Hair-level consequence (inference): the pigment of one shaft records the skin temperature during its growth. The mask, ears, paws and tail darken; body colour shifts with season.

## Gaps / uncertain
1. **Eye-area hair direction is unsourced.** This covers above and below the eye, the inner and outer canthi, temples, cheeks, whisker pads, chin, throat and the ear surfaces. Leave these as explicit assumptions in the engine, flagged for photo-based checking. Do not present them as anatomy.
2. **Where the nasal reversal starts is disputed.** Kidd puts it at a whorl midway between orbit and nostril (1900) or at a crest at orbit level (1903). No modern study was found.
3. **Body whorls are carnivore-general, not cat-specific.** The pectoral whorl and dorsal-forearm reversal come from Kidd, and the chest/axilla/elbow/ischial whorls from dog data.
4. **No cat hair density per cm²** by body region.
5. **No guard/awn/down diameters (µm)** for domestic cats; only one primary-shaft value (47 µm) and breed means of 23.5-25.6 µm.
6. **Several [B] items were not read in full.** Baker 1974 and Ryder 1976 (PDFs), Ohio State histology (blocked), VGL colorpoint page (403), Kaelin 2021 (secondary coverage only).
7. **Whisker counts per group are weakly sourced.** Only the carpal count (IMAIOS) is from an anatomy reference; the mystacial count is from popular sources plus Mivart's "about a dozen".
8. **No source on how dry cat fur forms locks.**
9. **The silver (Inhibitor) gene and the shaft-level colour gradient in pointed cats were not covered.**
