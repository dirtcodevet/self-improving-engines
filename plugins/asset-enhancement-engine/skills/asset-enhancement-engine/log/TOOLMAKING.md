# Tool-making record: how the measuring, mapping and checking tools came about

**Why this file exists.** The person, 2026-10-17:
- The custom measuring and mapping tools built for each problem, and the process that led to building and using them, are "one of the most valuable processes" in this project and "wickedly valuable to everything we do".
- Keep track of it so we can work out how to make it keep happening, **without mandating it as a criterion at gates**.

This file holds three things:
- the pattern (§1);
- the inventory, with what triggered each tool, what it caught and how good it turned out (§2);
- what separated the good tools from the flawed ones (§3).

§4 lists ideas for keeping the behaviour alive. It is input for the lessons assessment between Phase 1 and Phase 2; nothing in it is adopted yet.

Living document: every new tool gets a row in §2.

---

## 1. The pattern: the reasoning moves that produced the tools

These are observed from the history (LOG.md, METHOD.md, the tools' own docstrings), not reconstructed after the fact. Each move names tools it produced.

| # | Move | What it looks like | Produced / used |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Locate before fixing** | A surprise (a number, or the person's observation) gets a tool that says WHERE, WHICH PART, WHICH SIDE and HOW MUCH, before anything changes. | `diff_parts` (which part causes each silhouette miss); `seams` (v28 review creases attributed to the primitives that meet there); `region_error`; the v2 error attribution (by part, head frame, sign) that found the throat gap; `rb_measure3` locating the ear-base pockets |
| M2 | **Measure the reference in its own terms first** | Map the target (atlas) before comparing anything to it. The person's rule: "measure / compare / map first, THEN refine". | `ref_measure`, `face_measure`, `face_map`, `atlas_face`, `atlas_ears`, `atlas_body`, `relief` |
| M3 | **Demonstrate every number on the model** | A section, overlay or marker drawn on the model; a number nobody has seen on the model is not trusted. | Ear cross-sections caught the axis bug (sections were empty). Showing markers led to the person catching the toe and nose dots. Viewport close-ups caught the collar/pocket defects |
| M4 | **Test the tester** | A check must FAIL on a planted or known defect. A measuring volume must put the reference's own vertices on its zero level. | `checks.py --plant/--prove`; `frame_checks` (A3: must fail on the known-bad v37r frame); `exact_sdf` self-check (0.003 mm mean); the two-sided check run on the known-bad v3b first (it found the 239 mm³ of voids) |
| M5 | **Two independent methods for one number** | Agreement between unrelated methods makes a value real; disagreement locates a method error. | Tail thickness: blob tracking against the 2 mm analysis. Exact volume (0.004872 m³) against the mesh's signed volume. The old volume against the mesh vertices exposed the 0.47 mm offset |
| M6 | **Ask what the check cannot see** | Every check has a blind side; name it and close it. | One-sided → two-sided (`rb_measure3`). Claims → what was actually built (`partition.ear_extent`). `seams` exists because `checks.coherence` only looked at the mid-trunk. `multiview` down-weights the unseen side |
| M7 | **One definition, shared** | A quantity used by several tools lives in one place. | `mouth_geom` (five hand copies had drifted). `partition` (two unrelated cuts left a gap). `hair_tracts` (one hair-direction prior for groom and field) |
| M8 | **Instrument the representation's assumptions** | When a representation assumes something, record per element how far the assumption holds. | Non-star fraction per loft station (up to 100% on the paws: the polar form was wrong there); part-ID colours and owner maps; per-station centres |
| M9 | **Let the data choose the parameters** | Resolutions, cut planes and stop points come from a measurement, not a guess. | Grid resolution from the fur spectrum (1.9 mm grooves against a 1 mm grid = aliasing). Neck plane = minimum-area cut through the measured throat notch. Ear stacks run until their sections are empty. Smoothing σ from the relief spectrum |
| M10 | **A fast unit loop for one region** | When an artefact survives fixes, isolate the region and iterate in seconds. | `part_lab` / `lab_profile` (seconds instead of a 25-minute build) |
| M11 | **Code proposes, a picker picks, code checks** | Engine-grounding G4. | `outline_candidates`, `grid_crop` |
| M12 | **Stop chasing a definition** | J5: after several definitions of the same feature fail, isolate and reconsider, rather than keep editing. | Toes: five definitions, then stopped; the toes came out of the rebuilt sections instead |

**Where the behaviour comes from (honest answer).** I can't introspect on whether a given tool came from the model or from the skills. What the record shows:

1. **The person's rules were the strongest trigger.** Most tools were built within a turn of one of these rules applying:
   - "measure first";
   - "demonstrate on the model / the only implausible is what can't be demonstrated";
   - "an independent reviewer on every measurement";
   - "don't patch: measure what went wrong, rebuild, retest".
2. **The engine-grounding skill is named in some tools.** Its items appear explicitly in `frame_fit` (G3), `frame_checks` (G2, A3/A5), `outline_candidates` and `grid_crop` (G4).
3. **The rest looks like default engineering habit:** locate, cross-check, test the tester.

So the triggers matter as much as the habit. A rule that forbids changing anything before measuring creates the demand for a measuring tool.

## 3. What separated the good tools from the flawed ones

**Every flawed measuring tool missed at least one of these three things:**

1. **Validation against ground truth or a known defect before use.**
   - `hires` was never checked against the mesh it came from.
   - The coverage check was never run on a case with a known gap.
2. **Comparison against the SOURCE, not a derived product.**
   - `rb_measure` v1/v2 compared against marching cubes of my own volumes, so it inherited their errors.
3. **Comparison in BOTH directions.**
   - One-sided checks report a fine number while extra or misplaced surface goes unseen: 0.03 mm reported while 6 mm pockets existed.

**The good tools share three traits:**
- a self-check built in (`exact_sdf`, `checks.py --prove`);
- attribution of each error to a part, side and place (`diff_parts`, `seams`, `rb_measure3`);
- a demonstration on the model that the person can see.

**A fifth, after REVIEW_layers_whiskers:** a self-test that cannot see the bug it exists for. The brute-force check skipped exactly the near-surface points where the sign bug lived. Re-creating the old bug on purpose exposed it (LOG #57). A test is only proven against the specific failure it is meant to catch.

**A sixth, also from REVIEW_layers_whiskers:** a "minor" rating is minor only for the quantity it was judged against. A 0.06 mm sign offset was minor for shape and the whole signal for 0.03 mm fur. When a new, smaller quantity is measured, old "minor" items get re-rated (J13).

**A fourth miss joined the list after REVIEW_v3:** a metric that cannot MOVE when the thing it measures breaks. The field-based number survived a deleted leg. The test is to break the thing on purpose and check that the number jumps (`twosided.prove`).

**From the person's viewport checks (LOG #71):** a curve built by joining points from separate measurements (cut-by-cut ends) can fly through air even when every point lies on the surface. Draw only curves that are themselves traced ON the surface (iso-lines, creases, rims), and look at them up close before showing them.

**From the paws session (a parallel builder):** its tools were better than mine for hollow and convex features, because it split classes at measured gaps, defined outlines at the overflow level, and checked plant proofs against an independent re-derivation. Parallel builders produce tools worth sharing, and an index of tools across sessions (§4.4) matters.

**The cost of a flawed tool was always time-delayed.** It reported confidently good numbers until something independent (the reviewer, the person's eye, a second method) disagreed.

## 4. Keeping it happening without gating it

Ideas for the lessons assessment; **none adopted**, each would be a toggle.

1. **Keep this record live.**
   - One row per new tool: trigger, caught, verdict.
   - The habit of putting "Why (measured, LOG #n)" in each tool's docstring already does half of this.
2. **Keep the triggers, not a tool quota.** The person's rules produced most tools: measure first, demonstrate on the model, an independent reviewer, don't patch. Keeping those rules keeps the demand for tools without a gate that says "build a tool".
3. **Moves as guidance, not criteria.** §1 could become a short "when something surprises you" list in the skill: locate → measure the source → two methods → test the tester → what can't it see? It would be heuristics a builder reaches for, not boxes checked at a gate.
4. **A tool index read at the start of a task**, so existing tools are found and reused (106 scripts exist). Reuse before rebuild; when a tool is replaced, its row says why.
5. **Narrow review briefs.** One tool per reviewer, so reviews return in minutes rather than about 33 minutes and several can run side by side (person, LOG #53). Keep working on what a review does not touch while it runs.
6. **The reviewer's standing question:** "what can this check not see, and was it tested on a known defect?" This is the one question that would have caught all three flawed-tool classes in §3.
7. **Feed it to the engine during the lessons assessment** (the person's phase plan). Decide which moves belong in the engine-grounding skill (domain-neutral) and which in the felid pack, and keep each switchable.

## 2026-10-18 cat v3, fine-grid: skin_build_v3.py (tripo_rebuild/), the resolution contract (A20)

- **Need.** Root cause 1 of V3_BRIEF: the features were probed at 0.25-0.3 mm but meshed on the 0.5 mm grid. The mouth slot, the pocket slot and the nostril open ends could not exist at that grid, and v2 fill_voids sealed what was left of them.
- **Tool.** `skin_build_v3.py` imports `skin_build.py` and changes nothing in it. It holds:
  - `CONTRACT`, `ratio_for` and `contract_check`: what grid each feature needs, and a check that fails a REGION meshed coarser than that.
  - `merge`: a 0.5 mm body plus fine boxes, which are blocks of coarse cells refined r times.
  - `fill_voids_v3` (field level) and `fill_voids_mesh` (mesh level, for the fine boxes): both refuse to fill a void that touches an opening.
  - `opening_sdf_head`: the openings, taken from each feature's own fields.
  - `integrity`: the crack and shell check.
  - `cat_fine_field` and `cat_band_fn`: mode A for the cat.
  - `upsample_error`, `real_demo` and `project_head`.
- **The choice: why it is crack-free.** The fine box faces lie on coarse planes. On those faces the fine field is the trilinear interpolant of the coarse field; inside, it ramps to the true fine field over one coarse voxel. Along a coarse edge that interpolant is linear, so the fine vertices there are exactly the coarse vertices (the anchors). Between two anchors the fine contour lies on one convex hyperbola arc, so the gap to the coarse chord is a convex polygon, which a fan of triangles closes.
- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - Transvoxel transition cells: more code, and a second MC table.
  - Cutting both meshes on the plane and zipping them by arc length: fragile when the two sides disagree on topology.
  - One global fine grid: does not fit in memory.
- **Mode A instead of B.** Mode B re-runs the whole v2 method at the fine voxel. In v2's slab layout one minimal chunk needs 20 GB at 0.15 mm and 34 GB at 0.125 mm (estimated), which does not fit. Mode A upsamples the smooth base with Keys cubic and evaluates the edits and openings at the fine nodes.
- **Proofs (A22, both sides).**

  - Voids: a masked cavity is kept and an unmasked bubble is filled, at field level and at mesh level.
- **Real data.** One 6 mm box at the left mouth corner at 0.10 mm, built on the Phase 2 cores: 0 boundary edges, 102/102 chains stitched, anchors within 1.1e-6 voxel.

  - The real box found a 16-triangle, 7e-5 mm³ pinch-off of the mouth-slot air near the commissure. It sits inside the opening mask, so it was kept and the shell check failed.
